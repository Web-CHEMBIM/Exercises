10. B (Increases: LDR resistance increases in dark → V₂ increases)

SECTION B: TRUE/FALSE
11. FALSE – Voltage splits in the same ratio as the resistances;
    the larger resistor has the larger p.d.
12. TRUE
13. TRUE
14. TRUE – As temperature increases, thermistor R₁ decreases, so
    a larger fraction of voltage appears across R₂.

SECTION C: FILL-IN-THE-BLANK
15. increases
16. ratio; resistances
17. increase
18. E (the total e.m.f.)

SECTION D: MATCHING
19. A = 2, B = 1, C = 4, D = 3
20. A = 2, B = 4, C = 3, D = 1

SECTION E: SHORT ANSWER
21. V = IR: for constant I, if R increases then V increases.
22. A potential divider uses two resistors in series to split the
    voltage from a power source, allowing a fraction of the total
    voltage to be obtained.
23. Since the current I is the same through both (series), V = IR
    means the resistor with greater R has greater V.
24. (a) Light increases → LDR resistance decreases → V₂ decreases.
    (b) Light decreases → LDR resistance increases → V₂ increases.
25. Changing the variable resistor changes the resistance ratio, which
    changes how the voltage is divided. This adjusts the p.d. across
    the lamp, controlling its brightness.

SECTION F: CALCULATIONS
26. (a) R₁:R₂ = 6:18 = 1:3
    (b) V₁ = 12 × 1/4 = 3 V; V₂ = 12 × 3/4 = 9 V
    (c) 3 + 9 = 12 V ✓
27. (a) R₁/R₂ = V₁/V₂ → 200/300 = V₁/V₂ → V₁/V₂ = 2/3
        V₁ + V₂ = 10; V₁ = 2/5 × 10 = 4 V
    (b) V₂ = 10 − 4 = 6 V
28. (a) I = V/R = 8/(20+60) = 8/80 = 0.10 A
    (b) V₁ = 0.10 × 20 = 2 V; V₂ = 0.10 × 60 = 6 V
    (c) R₁/R₂ = 20/60 = 1/3; V₁/V₂ = 2/6 = 1/3 ✓
29. (a) R₁/R₂ = V₁/V₂ → 2/8 = V₁/4.0 → V₁ = 1.0 V
    (b) V_in = 1.0 + 4.0 = 5.0 V
30. (a) V₂ = 5 × 4/(1+4) = 5 × 4/5 = 4.0 V
    (b) V₂ = 5 × 4/(0.2+4) = 5 × 4/4.2 = 4.76 V
    (c) As temperature increases, thermistor resistance decreases,
        changing the ratio and thus changing V₂.
