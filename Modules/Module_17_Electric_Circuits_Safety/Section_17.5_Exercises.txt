================================================================================
                    SECTION 17.5 EXERCISES
                    ELECTRICAL SAFETY
================================================================================

Learning Objectives Covered:
  * State the hazards of damaged insulation, overheating cables, damp
    conditions, and excess current from overloading.
  * Know that a mains circuit consists of a live wire, neutral wire and
    earth wire; explain why a switch must be connected to the live wire.
  * Explain the use and operation of trip switches and fuses.
  * Explain why the outer casing of an electrical appliance must be either
    non-conducting (double-insulated) or earthed.
  * State that a fuse without an earth wire protects the circuit and
    cabling for a double-insulated appliance.

Total Questions: 50
Distribution:
  - AO1 (Recall):      13 questions (26%)
  - AO2 (Application): 25 questions (50%)
  - AO3 (Analysis):    12 questions (24%)

    path for fault current to blow fuse; e.g. washing machine, kettle.
    Double insulation: plastic/non-conducting casing; no earth needed;
    casing cannot become live; e.g. hair dryer, phone charger.

SECTION L: PREDICT AND EXPLAIN
48. Prediction: If a live wire touches the metal casing, the user
    could receive an electric shock.
    Explanation: Without the earth wire, there is no safe path for
    fault current. The metal casing becomes live. If the user touches
    the casing, current flows through their body to earth, causing
    injury or death. The fuse may not blow quickly enough.
49. Prediction: The 13 A fuse will not blow during the fault, and
    the wiring may overheat and cause a fire.
    Explanation: A 13 A fuse allows up to 13 A before blowing.
    A fault current of, say, 8 A is dangerous for a 2 A appliance
    but would not blow a 13 A fuse. The wires would overheat.
50. Prediction: The user is at high risk of electric shock.
    Explanation: Water conducts electricity. If water contacts any
    live component, current could flow through the water and through
    the user's body. Wet skin has much lower resistance than dry skin,
    increasing the current through the body and the severity of shock.
