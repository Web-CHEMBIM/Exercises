                    SECTION 18.1: ELECTROMAGNETIC INDUCTION
================================================================================

18.1.1 INDUCED E.M.F.
---------------------

An electromotive force (e.m.f.) is induced in a conductor whenever there is
RELATIVE MOVEMENT between the conductor and a magnetic field.

This can occur when:
1. The conductor moves in a stationary magnetic field
2. The conductor is stationary in a changing magnetic field

KEY PRINCIPLE:
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  For an e.m.f. to be induced, the conductor must CUT through magnetic       │
│  field lines. No cutting = no induced e.m.f.                                │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

CASE 1: Moving Conductor in Fixed Field
• When a wire moves through a magnetic field, it cuts the field lines
• An e.m.f. is induced in the wire
• If the wire is part of a complete circuit, a current will flow

CASE 2: Changing Field Around Stationary Conductor
• When a magnet moves through a coil, the field lines cut through the turns
• An e.m.f. is induced in each turn of the coil
• The total induced e.m.f. depends on the number of turns

18.1.2 FACTORS AFFECTING THE MAGNITUDE OF INDUCED E.M.F.
--------------------------------------------------------

The SIZE of the induced e.m.f. can be increased by:

1. SPEED: Moving the wire, coil or magnet FASTER
   → More field lines cut per second

2. NUMBER OF TURNS: Adding more turns to the coil
   → Each turn cuts field lines, contributing to total e.m.f.

3. COIL AREA: Increasing the area of the coils
   → More wire to cut through field lines

4. FIELD STRENGTH: Using a stronger magnet
   → More field lines in a given area

The DIRECTION of the induced e.m.f. depends on:
1. The orientation of the magnetic poles
2. The direction of movement

Reversing either of these will reverse the direction of the induced e.m.f.

18.1.3 LENZ'S LAW [SUPPLEMENT]
------------------------------

Lenz's law states:

┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  The direction of an induced e.m.f. always OPPOSES the change causing it.  │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

This means:
• Any magnetic field created by an induced current will act to STOP the motion
  that caused it
• This is a consequence of energy conservation

EXAMPLE: Magnet approaching a coil
When a N-pole is pushed INTO a coil:
• The end of the coil nearest the magnet becomes a N-POLE
• This REPELS the approaching magnet, opposing its motion

When a N-pole is pulled AWAY from a coil:
• The end of the coil nearest the magnet becomes a S-POLE
• This ATTRACTS the departing magnet, opposing its motion

18.1.4 RIGHT-HAND DYNAMO RULE [SUPPLEMENT]
------------------------------------------

When a wire moves through a magnetic field, the direction of the induced e.m.f.
can be determined using the RIGHT-HAND DYNAMO RULE:

┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  First finger = Field direction (N to S)                                   │
│  Thumb = Motion of conductor                                               │
│  Second finger = Direction of induced Current (or e.m.f.)                  │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

Hold your RIGHT hand with thumb, first finger and second finger mutually
perpendicular (at right angles to each other).

18.2.1 STRUCTURE OF A SIMPLE A.C. GENERATOR
--------------------------------------------

An a.c. generator (alternator) converts KINETIC ENERGY into ELECTRICAL ENERGY.

An alternating e.m.f. is generated, which causes an alternating current to flow.

COMPONENTS OF A SIMPLE A.C. GENERATOR:

┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  Component         │  Function                                             │
├────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤

│  Rotating coil     │  Cuts magnetic field lines as it rotates              │
│  Slip rings        │  Allow alternating current to flow to external circuit│
│  Carbon brushes    │  Maintain electrical connection with rotating rings   │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

18.2.2 OPERATION OF AN A.C. GENERATOR
--------------------------------------

As the coil rotates in the magnetic field:

1. The coil cuts through magnetic field lines
2. An e.m.f. is induced in the coil (electromagnetic induction)
3. The slip rings and brushes transfer this e.m.f. to the external circuit
4. If the circuit is complete, an alternating current flows

WHY THE CURRENT ALTERNATES:
• As the coil rotates through 360°, each side alternately moves up then down
• This reverses the direction of the induced e.m.f. every half-turn
• The result is an alternating current

18.2.3 E.M.F. VS ANGLE GRAPHS [SUPPLEMENT]
------------------------------------------

The induced e.m.f. varies as the coil rotates:

MAXIMUM E.M.F. occurs when:
• The coil is HORIZONTAL (parallel to the field)
• The coil is moving PERPENDICULAR to the field
• Maximum number of field lines are being cut

ZERO E.M.F. occurs when:
• The coil is VERTICAL (perpendicular to the field)
• The coil is moving PARALLEL to the field
• No field lines are being cut

18.3.1 MAGNETIC FIELD AROUND A STRAIGHT WIRE
----------------------------------------------

When current flows through a wire, a magnetic field is produced around it.

FIELD PATTERN AROUND A STRAIGHT WIRE:
• The field lines are CONCENTRIC CIRCLES centered on the wire
• The wire is at the center of these circles
• There are no poles (unlike a bar magnet)

RIGHT-HAND GRIP RULE:
To find the direction of the field:
• Point your RIGHT THUMB in the direction of the current
• Your CURLED FINGERS show the direction of the field lines

• If the current direction is reversed, the field direction also reverses
• Field lines would go clockwise instead of anticlockwise (or vice versa)

18.3.2 MAGNETIC FIELD AROUND A SOLENOID
----------------------------------------

A SOLENOID is a coil of wire, like a spring or helix.

When current flows through a solenoid, it creates a magnetic field similar to
a BAR MAGNET:
• One end becomes a NORTH pole
• The other end becomes a SOUTH pole
• Field lines emerge from N and enter at S

18.3.3 APPLICATIONS: RELAYS AND LOUDSPEAKERS
---------------------------------------------

RELAY CIRCUITS:

A relay is a switch operated by an electromagnet. It allows a small current in
one circuit to control a larger current in another circuit.

How a relay works:
1. Current flows through the coil (Circuit 1), creating a magnetic field
2. The magnetic field attracts an iron armature
3. The armature pivots and closes contacts in Circuit 2
4. Current can now flow in Circuit 2

When Circuit 1 is switched off:
• The magnetic field disappears
• The armature returns to its original position (by a spring)
• Circuit 2 is broken

LOUDSPEAKERS:

A loudspeaker converts electrical signals into sound waves.

How a loudspeaker works:
1. An alternating current flows through a coil wrapped around a magnet
2. The changing current produces a changing magnetic field in the coil
3. This field interacts with the permanent magnet's field
4. Forces are produced that make the coil oscillate back and forth
5. The coil is attached to a cone that vibrates, producing sound waves

18.3.4 EFFECT OF CHANGING CURRENT [SUPPLEMENT]
----------------------------------------------

INCREASING CURRENT:
• Makes the magnetic field STRONGER
• Field lines become CLOSER TOGETHER

REVERSING CURRENT DIRECTION:
• Reverses the direction of the magnetic field
• N and S poles swap positions

For a solenoid with iron core (electromagnet):
• Increasing current increases field strength
• Adding more turns increases field strength
• Reversing current reverses the poles

                    SECTION 18.4: FORCE ON A CURRENT-CARRYING CONDUCTOR
================================================================================

18.4.1 THE MOTOR EFFECT
------------------------

When a current-carrying conductor is placed in a magnetic field, it experiences
a FORCE. This is called the MOTOR EFFECT.

Conditions for the force:
• The conductor must be carrying a current
• The conductor must be in an external magnetic field
• The current direction must NOT be parallel to the field

The force is greatest when the current is PERPENDICULAR to the field.

REVERSING THE FORCE:
The direction of the force can be reversed by:
1. Reversing the direction of the current, OR
2. Reversing the direction of the magnetic field

Reversing BOTH will keep the force in the same direction.

18.4.2 FLEMING'S LEFT-HAND RULE [SUPPLEMENT]
--------------------------------------------

Fleming's Left-Hand Rule determines the direction of the force on a
current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field.

Using your LEFT hand:
• First finger = Field direction (N to S)
• SeCond finger = Current direction (conventional: + to −)
• ThuMb = Motion (direction of Force/Thrust)

WORKED EXAMPLE 18.1
A wire carrying current from left to right is placed in a magnetic field
pointing from north (top) to south (bottom). In which direction will the
force act on the wire?

Solution:
Using Fleming's left-hand rule:
• First finger points down (field direction: N to S)
• Second finger points right (current direction)
• Thumb points OUT OF THE PAGE

The force acts out of the page (toward you).

18.4.3 FORCE ON CHARGED PARTICLES [SUPPLEMENT]
----------------------------------------------

A current is a flow of charged particles. When a charged particle moves through
a magnetic field, it experiences a force.

For a beam of ELECTRONS:
• Electrons move from negative to positive
• Conventional current is in the OPPOSITE direction
• So point the second finger OPPOSITE to electron motion

The force on the particle is always at 90° to both:
• The direction of motion
• The direction of the magnetic field

Result: The particle follows a CURVED PATH in the field.

2. First finger = left to right (field), Second finger = upward (current),
   Thumb = INTO the page. Force acts into the page.
3. Moving electrons carry charge, which is equivalent to a current. The magnetic
   field exerts a force on the moving charges at right angles to their motion,
   causing them to follow a curved path.

18.5.1 THE TURNING EFFECT
--------------------------

A current-carrying coil placed in a magnetic field experiences a TURNING EFFECT
(also called a torque or couple).

This is because:
• Each side of the coil carries current in OPPOSITE directions
• Using Fleming's left-hand rule, forces act in OPPOSITE directions on each side
• These opposite forces create a turning effect

FACTORS THAT INCREASE THE TURNING EFFECT:
1. Increasing the NUMBER OF TURNS on the coil
2. Increasing the CURRENT through the coil
3. Increasing the STRENGTH of the magnetic field

18.5.2 STRUCTURE OF A D.C. MOTOR
---------------------------------

A simple d.c. motor consists of:
• A rotating coil between the poles of a permanent magnet
• A SPLIT-RING COMMUTATOR connected to the coil
• BRUSHES connecting the commutator to the power supply

HOW THE SPLIT-RING COMMUTATOR WORKS:

1. HORIZONTAL POSITION:
   • Current flows through the coil
   • Forces act on each side (opposite directions)
   • Coil rotates

2. VERTICAL POSITION:
   • The gap in the split ring aligns with the brushes
   • NO current flows (circuit broken momentarily)
   • Momentum carries the coil past this point

3. PAST VERTICAL:
   • The split ring reconnects, but sides have SWAPPED
   • Current now flows in the OPPOSITE direction through each side of the coil
   • But because the coil has rotated, the forces still push the same way
   • Rotation continues in the same direction

This ensures CONTINUOUS rotation in one direction.

CHANGING MOTOR BEHAVIOR:
• To reverse direction: reverse current OR reverse magnetic field
• To increase speed: increase current OR use stronger magnets
• To increase force: increase current, stronger field, or more turns

18.6.1 STRUCTURE OF A TRANSFORMER

A transformer changes the size of an alternating voltage (and current).

A simple transformer consists of:
• A PRIMARY COIL: connected to the input (a.c. supply)

WHY SOFT IRON?
• Iron is easily magnetised and demagnetised
• This allows the magnetic field to change rapidly with the a.c. input
• "Soft" means magnetically soft (not physically soft)

18.6.2 STEP-UP AND STEP-DOWN TRANSFORMERS
------------------------------------------

STEP-UP TRANSFORMER:
• Increases voltage (Vs > Vp)
• Has MORE turns on secondary than primary (Ns > Np)
• Decreases current (Is < Ip) to conserve power

STEP-DOWN TRANSFORMER:
• Decreases voltage (Vs < Vp)
• Has FEWER turns on secondary than primary (Ns < Np)
• Increases current (Is > Ip) to conserve power

18.6.3 THE TRANSFORMER EQUATION
--------------------------------

The relationship between voltages and turns is given by:

┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                         Vp     Np                                           │
│                         ── = ──                                             │
│                         Vs     Ns                                           │
│                                                                             │
│  OR equivalently:       Vs     Ns                                           │
│                         ── = ──                                             │
│                         Vp     Np                                           │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

Where:
• Vp = primary voltage (V)
• Vs = secondary voltage (V)
• Np = number of turns on primary coil
• Ns = number of turns on secondary coil

WORKED EXAMPLE 18.2
A transformer has 20 turns on the primary coil and 800 turns on the secondary
coil. The primary voltage is 500 V. Calculate the secondary voltage.

Solution:
Vp/Vs = Np/Ns

Rearranging: Vs = Vp × (Ns/Np)

Vs = 500 × (800/20)
Vs = 500 × 40
Vs = 20,000 V

This is a step-up transformer (Vs > Vp, Ns > Np).

18.6.4 PRINCIPLE OF OPERATION [SUPPLEMENT]
------------------------------------------

How a transformer works:

1. An alternating current flows through the primary coil
2. This creates a changing magnetic field in the primary coil
3. The soft iron core is easily magnetised, so the changing field passes
   through it
4. This changing magnetic field links with the secondary coil
5. By electromagnetic induction, an e.m.f. is induced in the secondary coil
6. The induced e.m.f. is also alternating (same frequency as input)
7. If the secondary is part of a complete circuit, an a.c. flows

IMPORTANT: Transformers only work with a.c. because:
• A steady (d.c.) current produces a constant magnetic field
• A constant field does not induce an e.m.f. in the secondary
• The field must be CHANGING to induce an e.m.f.

18.6.5 IDEAL TRANSFORMER EQUATION [SUPPLEMENT]
----------------------------------------------

For a 100% efficient (ideal) transformer, power in = power out:

┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                         IpVp = IsVs                                         │
│                                                                             │
│  (primary power = secondary power)                                          │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

Where:
• Ip = primary current (A)
• Vp = primary voltage (V)
• Is = secondary current (A)
• Vs = secondary voltage (V)

This can be rearranged to:

Is/Ip = Vp/Vs

This shows that:
• If voltage INCREASES (step-up), current DECREASES
• If voltage DECREASES (step-down), current INCREASES

WORKED EXAMPLE 18.3
A transformer steps up 115 V to 230 V. A current of 5 A flows through the
secondary coil. Calculate the current in the primary coil (assuming 100%
efficiency).

Solution:
IpVp = IsVs

Ip × 115 = 5 × 230
Ip × 115 = 1150
Ip = 1150 / 115
Ip = 10 A

The primary current is 10 A (larger than secondary, as expected for step-up).

18.6.6 HIGH-VOLTAGE TRANSMISSION
---------------------------------

Electricity is transmitted at HIGH VOLTAGE and LOW CURRENT because:

Power loss in cables is given by:

┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                         P = I²R                                             │
│                                                                             │
│  Power loss depends on current SQUARED                                      │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

Since power = voltage × current (P = VI), for the same power:
• Higher voltage → Lower current
• Lower current → Much less power loss (since P ∝ I²)

THE TRANSMISSION SYSTEM:

1. Power station generates electricity at moderate voltage (~25 kV)
2. STEP-UP transformer increases voltage for transmission (~400 kV)
3. Electricity travels through transmission lines at high voltage, low current
4. STEP-DOWN transformer reduces voltage for distribution (~33 kV, 11 kV)
5. Further step-down transformers reduce to mains voltage (~230 V)

ADVANTAGES OF HIGH-VOLTAGE TRANSMISSION:
• Less energy wasted as heat in cables (more efficient)
• Lower currents allow thinner (cheaper) cables to be used

KEY CONCEPTS:

ELECTROMAGNETIC INDUCTION (18.1):
• E.m.f. induced when conductor cuts magnetic field lines
• Factors affecting magnitude: speed, turns, field strength, coil area
• Lenz's law: induced e.m.f. opposes the change causing it
• Right-hand dynamo rule: relates field, motion, and induced current

A.C. GENERATOR (18.2):
• Components: rotating coil, permanent magnet, slip rings, brushes
• E.m.f. maximum when coil horizontal, zero when vertical
• Output is alternating (sine wave)

MAGNETIC EFFECT OF CURRENT (18.3):
• Straight wire: concentric circular field lines
• Solenoid: field like bar magnet (N and S poles)
• Right-hand grip rule for field direction
• Applications: relays, loudspeakers, electromagnets

MOTOR EFFECT (18.4):
• Current-carrying conductor in field experiences force
• Fleming's left-hand rule: First=Field, SeCond=Current, ThuMb=Force
• Force on charged particles causes curved paths

D.C. MOTOR (18.5):
• Split-ring commutator reverses current every half turn
• Ensures continuous rotation in one direction
• Increase turning effect: more turns, more current, stronger field

TRANSFORMERS (18.6):
• Vp/Vs = Np/Ns (voltage-turns ratio)
• Step-up: Ns > Np, increases voltage
• Step-down: Ns < Np, decreases voltage
• IpVp = IsVs (for ideal transformer)
• High-voltage transmission reduces power loss (P = I²R)

KEY EQUATIONS:

Transformer equation:      Vp/Vs = Np/Ns

Ideal transformer:         IpVp = IsVs

Power loss in cables:      P = I²R
