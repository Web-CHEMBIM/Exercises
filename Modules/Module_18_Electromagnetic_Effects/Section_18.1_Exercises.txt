================================================================================
                         MARK SCHEME
================================================================================

1. (i) A conductor moves through (cuts) a magnetic field / field lines.
   (ii) A changing (varying) magnetic field links with a stationary conductor.

2. (a) An e.m.f. (and current) has been induced in the coil.
   (b) The reading returns to zero. No relative movement between magnet
       and coil, so no field lines are cut and no e.m.f. is induced.
   (c) The needle deflects to the left (opposite direction).

3. (i) Increase speed of movement.
   (ii) Increase number of turns on the coil.
   (iii) Use a stronger magnet (increase field strength).
   (iv) Increase the area of the coil.
   (Any 4 of the above.)

4. (a) The induced e.m.f. is directly proportional to the speed — as speed
       doubles, e.m.f. doubles.
   (b) 20 mV.

5. (a) 4 V (doubling turns doubles e.m.f.).
   (b) Each turn cuts through the field lines; more turns means more turns
       cutting lines per second, so total induced e.m.f. is greater.

6. Not fully correct. An e.m.f. can also be induced when the conductor is
   stationary if the magnetic field around it is changing (e.g., a moving
   magnet near a stationary coil).

7. The direction of an induced e.m.f. always opposes the change that caused it.

8. (a) The near end becomes a north pole (repels the approaching magnet).
   (b) Energy must be supplied to push the magnet in against the repulsive force.
       This work done is converted to electrical energy — energy is conserved.

9. (a) The near end becomes a south pole (attracts the departing magnet).
   (b) The south pole tries to pull the magnet back / opposes its removal,
       opposing the change causing the induction.

10. First finger = field direction (north to south / pointing south).
    Thumb = motion (upward).
    Second finger (induced current direction) points out of the page
    (or determined by the mutual perpendicular orientation of the hand).
    (Accept correct use of right-hand dynamo rule with correct conclusion.)
