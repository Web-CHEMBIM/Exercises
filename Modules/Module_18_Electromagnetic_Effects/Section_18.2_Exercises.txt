1. (i) Permanent magnet. (ii) Rotating coil. (iii) Slip rings. (iv) Carbon brushes.

2. (a) Slip rings: allow the alternating current to pass from the rotating coil
       to the external circuit. They rotate with the coil.
   (b) Carbon brushes: maintain a continuous electrical connection with the
       slip rings while stationary. Allow current to flow to the external circuit.

3. From: kinetic energy (mechanical energy). To: electrical energy.

4. As the coil rotates, each side alternately moves up then down through the field.
   This reverses the direction of the induced e.m.f. every half turn.
   The current therefore repeatedly changes direction, producing alternating current.

5. Slip rings are complete (continuous) rings — one for each end of the coil.
   The split-ring commutator is a single ring split into two halves with gaps between them.
   (Or: slip rings allow a.c. to flow; the commutator reverses current to maintain d.c. rotation.)

6. (i) Increase the speed of rotation.
   (ii) Increase the number of turns on the coil.
   (iii) Use a stronger permanent magnet.
   (Accept also: insert a soft iron core into the coil.)

7. (a) At 90° (and 270°).
   (b) At 0°, 180°, and 360°.
   (c) The coil is horizontal (parallel to the magnetic field). At this position, the
       coil sides are moving perpendicular to the field, cutting the maximum number of
       field lines per second, so the induced e.m.f. is greatest.
   (d) The coil is vertical (perpendicular to the field). The coil sides move parallel
       to the field at this instant, cutting no field lines, so no e.m.f. is induced.

8. (a) The peak e.m.f. increases — at higher speed, more field lines are cut per second,
       so the maximum rate of cutting is greater.
   (b) The frequency doubles — the coil completes twice as many rotations per second,
       so the current reverses direction twice as often.

9. (a) 4 V.
   (b) Doubling the number of turns means twice as much wire cuts through the field
       in each rotation, doubling the induced e.m.f.

10. Sine wave starting at zero, rising to peak at 90°, back to zero at 180°,
    falling to trough at 270°, back to zero at 360°.
    Labelled: coil is vertical at 0°/180°/360° (zero e.m.f.);
              coil is horizontal at 90°/270° (peak/trough e.m.f.).
