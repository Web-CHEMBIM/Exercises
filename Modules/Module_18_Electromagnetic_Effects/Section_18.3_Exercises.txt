================================================================================
                         MARK SCHEME
================================================================================

1. Shape: concentric circles centred on the wire.
   Direction: anticlockwise (for current out of the page) — use right-hand grip rule.

2. Rule name: right-hand grip rule.
   How to apply: point the right thumb in the direction of the (conventional) current;
   the curled fingers show the direction of the magnetic field lines around the wire.

3. (a) The field outside the solenoid is similar to a bar magnet: oval / curved lines
       emerging from one end (north pole) and entering the other end (south pole).
       Inside, the field lines are straight and parallel (uniform field).
   (b) The solenoid acts just like a bar magnet with a north and south pole.
   (c) (i) Increase the current. (ii) Increase the number of turns.
       (Accept also: insert a soft iron core.)

4. (a) The magnetic field strength increases significantly.
   (b) An electromagnet.
   (c) Soft iron is easily magnetised and demagnetised. When the current is switched
       off, the soft iron loses its magnetism quickly, so the electromagnet can be
       switched on and off easily. Steel would remain magnetised.

5. A small current in Circuit 1 flows through the coil of the electromagnet.
   This creates a magnetic field which attracts the iron armature.
   The armature pivots and closes the contacts in Circuit 2.
   This allows the much larger current in Circuit 2 to flow through the load.
   (When Circuit 1 is switched off, the spring returns the armature, breaking Circuit 2.)

6. (i) Car starter motor (switch). (ii) Electric bell.
   (Accept also: electronic locks, scrapyard crane control.)

7. An alternating current flows through a coil wrapped around a permanent magnet.
   The changing current produces a changing magnetic field in the coil.
   This changing field interacts with the permanent magnet, producing alternating forces.
   The coil (and attached cone/diaphragm) vibrates back and forth, creating sound waves.

8. (a) The field strength increases — more current means more magnetic effect per turn,
       producing a stronger field.
   (b) Field lines are drawn closer together to represent the stronger field.

9. (a) The direction of the magnetic field reverses.
   (b) The north and south poles swap positions.

10. soft / iron / stronger / increases / reversed / direction
