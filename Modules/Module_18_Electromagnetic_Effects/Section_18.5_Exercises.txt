                         MARK SCHEME
================================================================================

1. A turning effect (torque / couple).

2. Each side of the coil carries current in opposite directions.
   Using Fleming's left-hand rule, the forces on each side act in opposite directions.
   These two opposite forces act at different points on the coil, creating a turning effect
   rather than a net linear force.

3. (i) Increase the number of turns on the coil.
   (ii) Increase the current through the coil.
   (iii) Use a stronger magnetic field (stronger magnets).

4. (i) Rotating coil. (ii) Split-ring commutator. (iii) Carbon brushes.

5. Motor uses: a split-ring commutator (two half-rings with gaps).
   Generator uses: slip rings (complete continuous rings).

6. (i) Reverse the direction of the current.
   (ii) Reverse the direction of the magnetic field (swap magnet poles).

7. (i) Increase the current.
   (ii) Use stronger magnets (increase field strength). (Accept: more turns.)

8. When the coil is vertical, the gap in the split ring aligns with the carbon brushes.
   This momentarily breaks the circuit so no current flows.
   The momentum of the coil carries it past this point.

9. When the coil passes the vertical position, the two halves of the split ring swap
   which brush they are in contact with.
   This reverses the direction of current flowing through the coil.
   Because the coil has also rotated 180°, the effect of the reversed current is that
   the forces on each side still act in the same rotational direction.
   This ensures continuous rotation in the same direction.

10. (a) The coil would rock back and forth / oscillate — it would not rotate continuously.
    (b) Slip rings maintain the same connection between each side of the coil and the
        supply. As the coil passes the vertical position, the forces on each side reverse
        direction (because the current direction relative to the field reverses), causing
        the coil to rotate back instead of continuing forward.
