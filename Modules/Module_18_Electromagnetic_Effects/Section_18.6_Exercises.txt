1. (i) Primary coil. (ii) Secondary coil. (iii) Soft iron core.

2. Soft iron is easily magnetised and demagnetised. This allows the magnetic field
   to change rapidly with the alternating current, which is essential for the
   changing field to induce an e.m.f. in the secondary.

3.
   Step-up:   Vs > Vp (voltage increases); Ns > Np (more turns on secondary).
   Step-down: Vs < Vp (voltage decreases); Ns < Np (fewer turns on secondary).

4. Vp/Vs = Np/Ns
   Vp = primary (input) voltage (V)
   Vs = secondary (output) voltage (V)
   Np = number of turns on the primary coil
   Ns = number of turns on the secondary coil

5. (a) Step-up, because Ns (500) > Np (100), so the secondary voltage is greater
       than the primary voltage.
   (b) Vp/Vs = Np/Ns → Vs = Vp × (Ns/Np) = 240 × (500/100) = 240 × 5 = 1200 V.

6. (a) Np/Ns = Vp/Vs → Ns = Np × (Vs/Vp) = 4400 × (230/11000) = 4400 × 0.0209 = 92 turns.
   (b) Step-down transformer.

7. Vp/Vs = Np/Ns → Vp = Vs × (Np/Ns) = 48 × (50/200) = 48 × 0.25 = 12 V.

8. Transformers work by electromagnetic induction, which requires a CHANGING
   magnetic field. Alternating current continuously changes direction, producing
   a continuously changing magnetic field in the primary coil. This changing field
   links with the secondary coil and induces an e.m.f.
   Direct current produces a constant magnetic field that does not change, so no
   e.m.f. is induced in the secondary. The transformer does not work.

9. IpVp = IsVs
   Ip = primary (input) current (A)
   Is = secondary (output) current (A)

10. (a) P = IV = 230 × 4 = 920 W.
    (b) IpVp = IsVs → Is = IpVp/Vs = (4 × 230)/46 = 920/46 = 20 A.
    (c) Yes. This is a step-down transformer (voltage decreases from 230 V to 46 V).
        For power to be conserved, the secondary current must be larger than the
        primary current. 20 A > 4 A, which is consistent with a step-down transformer.

11. (a) IpVp = IsVs → Is = IpVp/Vs = (800 × 25000)/400000 = 20,000,000/400,000 = 50 A.
    (b) Power loss = I²R. At 400,000 V, the current is only 50 A (much lower than 800 A
        at 25,000 V). Because power loss depends on I², a smaller current means
        vastly less power wasted as heat — (50)² = 2,500 vs (800)² = 640,000.
        High-voltage transmission is therefore much more efficient.

12. (a) At 10,000 V: P = (100)² × 2 = 20,000 W.
        At 100,000 V: P = (10)² × 2 = 200 W.
    (b) Transmitting at 10 times the voltage reduces the current by a factor of 10,
        which reduces the power loss by a factor of 100 (since P ∝ I²). High-voltage
        transmission results in far less energy wasted as heat in the cables.
