19.1.1 ATOMIC STRUCTURE
------------------------

Atoms are the building blocks of all matter.

STRUCTURE OF AN ATOM:
• A small, dense, POSITIVELY CHARGED NUCLEUS at the center
• NEGATIVELY CHARGED ELECTRONS in orbit around the nucleus

KEY FACTS ABOUT ATOMS:
• Atoms are incredibly small: radius ≈ 1 × 10⁻¹⁰ m
• About 100 million atoms could fit across your thumbnail
• The nucleus is over 10,000 times smaller than the whole atom
• The nucleus contains almost all the mass of the atom
• Most of the atom is EMPTY SPACE

19.1.2 ATOMS AND IONS
----------------------

An ION is an electrically charged atom formed by losing or gaining electrons.

┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  NEUTRAL ATOM:  Number of protons = Number of electrons                    │
│                 Net charge = 0                                              │
│                                                                             │
│  POSITIVE ION:  Atom LOSES electrons                                        │
│                 More protons than electrons                                 │
│                 Net positive charge                                         │
│                                                                             │
│  NEGATIVE ION:  Atom GAINS electrons                                        │
│                 More electrons than protons                                 │
│                 Net negative charge                                         │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

WHY DO ATOMS FORM IONS?
• Atoms lose or gain electrons to become more stable
• The nucleus (protons and neutrons) does not change when forming ions
• Only the number of electrons changes

EXAMPLE:
• A sodium atom has 11 protons and 11 electrons (neutral)
• If it loses 1 electron, it has 11 protons and 10 electrons
• Net charge = +11 + (−10) = +1 → positive ion (Na⁺)

19.1.3 RUTHERFORD'S ALPHA SCATTERING EXPERIMENT [SUPPLEMENT]
------------------------------------------------------------

In 1909, Ernest Rutherford and his students Hans Geiger and Ernest Marsden
performed an experiment that provided evidence for the nuclear model.

THE EXPERIMENT:
• A beam of alpha particles (α-particles) was fired at thin gold foil
• Alpha particles are helium nuclei (He²⁺): 2 protons + 2 neutrons
• Detectors measured where the alpha particles went after hitting the foil

OBSERVATIONS:

| Observation | Proportion | What it tells us |
|-------------|------------|------------------|
| Most α-particles passed straight through | Vast majority | Atom is mostly EMPTY SPACE |
| Some α-particles were deflected at small angles | Small number | Came close to positive nucleus, repelled |
| Very few α-particles bounced back (>90°) | Very few | Hit the tiny, dense, positive nucleus |

CONCLUSIONS:
1. The atom is mostly EMPTY SPACE (most α-particles pass through)
2. The nucleus is very SMALL (very few bounce back)
3. The nucleus is DENSE and contains most of the MASS
4. The nucleus is POSITIVELY CHARGED (repels positive α-particles)

This experiment disproved the earlier "Plum Pudding" model and established
the nuclear model of the atom.

19.2.1 COMPOSITION OF THE NUCLEUS
----------------------------------

The nucleus is composed of:
• PROTONS: positively charged particles
• NEUTRONS: particles with no charge (neutral)

Together, protons and neutrons are called NUCLEONS.

The nucleus has an overall POSITIVE CHARGE because:
• Protons are positive
• Neutrons are neutral
• There are no electrons in the nucleus

19.2.2 RELATIVE CHARGES
------------------------

RELATIVE CHARGE compares the charge of particles to the fundamental charge.

┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  Particle     │  Relative Charge  │  Location                              │
├───────────────┼───────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────────┤
│  Proton       │       +1          │  Nucleus                               │
│  Neutron      │        0          │  Nucleus                               │
│  Electron     │       −1          │  Orbiting the nucleus                  │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

KEY POINTS:
• Protons and electrons have equal but opposite charges
• The actual charge of a proton = +1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C
• The actual charge of an electron = −1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C
• Relative charge is a ratio, so it has no units

19.2.3 PROTON NUMBER AND NUCLEON NUMBER
----------------------------------------

PROTON NUMBER (Z):
• Also called ATOMIC NUMBER
• The number of protons in an atom
• Determines which ELEMENT the atom is
• Also equals the number of electrons in a neutral atom

NUCLEON NUMBER (A):
• Also called MASS NUMBER
• The total number of protons + neutrons in the nucleus
• Determines the mass of the atom

CALCULATING NEUTRONS:
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  Number of neutrons = Nucleon number − Proton number                       │
│                     = A − Z                                                 │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

EXAMPLE:
Sodium-23 has nucleon number 23 and proton number 11.
• Number of neutrons = 23 − 11 = 12

19.2.4 NUCLIDE NOTATION
------------------------

Atoms are written in NUCLIDE NOTATION:

                    ᴬzX

Where:
• X = chemical symbol of the element
• A = nucleon number (top number)
• Z = proton number (bottom number)

EXAMPLE: Lithium-7

                    ⁷₃Li

• A = 7 (nucleon number: 3 protons + 4 neutrons)
• Z = 3 (proton number)
• Number of neutrons = 7 − 3 = 4
• Number of electrons in neutral atom = 3

WORKED EXAMPLE 19.1
How many protons, neutrons, and electrons are in ¹⁹⁷₇₉Au (gold)?

Solution:
• Proton number (Z) = 79 → 79 protons
• Nucleon number (A) = 197
• Number of neutrons = A − Z = 197 − 79 = 118 neutrons
• For a neutral atom: electrons = protons = 79 electrons

19.2.5 ISOTOPES
----------------

ISOTOPES are atoms of the SAME ELEMENT that have:
• The SAME number of PROTONS (same element)
• A DIFFERENT number of NEUTRONS

Because isotopes have different numbers of neutrons:
• They have the same proton number (Z)
• But different nucleon numbers (A)
• They have slightly different masses

EXAMPLE: Isotopes of Hydrogen

| Isotope | Protons | Neutrons | Nucleon Number | Nuclide Notation |
|---------|---------|----------|----------------|------------------|
| Hydrogen (protium) | 1 | 0 | 1 | ¹₁H |
| Deuterium | 1 | 1 | 2 | ²₁H |
| Tritium | 1 | 2 | 3 | ³₁H |

• Unstable isotopes have an imbalance of protons and neutrons
• More neutrons → often less stable → more likely to decay

19.2.6 RELATIVE CHARGE AND MASS OF NUCLEUS [SUPPLEMENT]
--------------------------------------------------------

NUCLEAR CHARGE:
The relative charge of a nucleus equals the proton number.

┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  Nuclear charge = Number of protons × (+1) = +Z                            │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

Example: Carbon (Z = 6) has nuclear charge = +6

NUCLEAR MASS:
The relative mass of a nucleus equals the nucleon number.

┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  Relative mass ≈ Number of nucleons = A                                    │
│                                                                             │
│  (Electron mass is negligible: about 1/2000 of a nucleon)                  │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

| Particle | Relative Mass |
|----------|---------------|
| Proton   | 1             |
| Neutron  | 1             |
| Electron | ≈ 1/2000 (negligible) |

Example: Carbon-12 has nucleon number 12, so relative mass ≈ 12

WORKED EXAMPLE 19.2
What is the relative charge and relative mass of a chromium nucleus ⁵²₂₄Cr?

Solution:
• Proton number Z = 24
• Nucleon number A = 52
• Relative charge = +24 (equals number of protons)
• Relative mass = 52 (equals nucleon number)

19.3.1 NUCLEAR FISSION [SUPPLEMENT]
------------------------------------

NUCLEAR FISSION is defined as:

┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  The splitting of a large, unstable nucleus into two smaller nuclei.       │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

HOW FISSION WORKS:
1. A NEUTRON collides with an unstable heavy nucleus (e.g., uranium-235)
2. The nucleus absorbs the neutron and becomes very unstable
3. The nucleus SPLITS into two smaller daughter nuclei
4. 2-3 NEUTRONS are released
5. GAMMA RAYS are also emitted
6. A large amount of ENERGY is released

EXAMPLE FISSION EQUATION:

²³⁵₉₂U + ¹₀n → ⁹²₃₆Kr + ¹⁴¹₅₆Ba + 3¹₀n + energy

CHECKING CONSERVATION:
• Nucleon numbers: 235 + 1 = 92 + 141 + 3(1) → 236 = 236 ✓
• Proton numbers: 92 + 0 = 36 + 56 + 3(0) → 92 = 92 ✓

MASS AND ENERGY:
• The total mass of products is slightly LESS than the mass of reactants
• This "missing mass" has been converted to ENERGY (E = mc²)
• This is the energy released in the fission process

APPLICATIONS:
• Nuclear power stations use fission of uranium-235 or plutonium-239
• The released neutrons can cause MORE fission → chain reaction

19.3.2 NUCLEAR FUSION [SUPPLEMENT]
-----------------------------------

NUCLEAR FUSION is defined as:

┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  The joining of two light nuclei to form a heavier nucleus.                │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

HOW FUSION WORKS:
1. Two light nuclei (e.g., hydrogen isotopes) are brought very close together
2. They must overcome electrostatic repulsion (both are positive)
3. At extremely high temperatures and pressures, they FUSE
4. A heavier nucleus is formed
5. A large amount of ENERGY is released

• Nucleon numbers: 2 + 1 = 3 ✓
• Proton numbers: 1 + 1 = 2 ✓

MASS AND ENERGY:
• The mass of the helium nucleus is slightly LESS than the total mass
  of the two hydrogen nuclei
• This "missing mass" is converted to energy

FUSION IN STARS:
• The Sun and other stars produce energy through nuclear fusion
• Hydrogen nuclei fuse to form helium
• Requires extremely high temperature (~15 million °C) and pressure
• The energy from fusion is what makes stars shine

WHY FUSION IS HARD ON EARTH:
• Requires extreme conditions (high temperature and pressure)
• Nuclei repel each other (both positively charged)
• Must overcome this repulsion to get close enough to fuse
• Currently difficult to achieve controlled fusion for power generation

19.3.3 COMPARING FISSION AND FUSION
------------------------------------

| Feature | Fission | Fusion |
|---------|---------|--------|
| Definition | Splitting a large nucleus | Joining light nuclei |
| Starting nuclei | Heavy (e.g., U-235, Pu-239) | Light (e.g., H-2, H-3) |
| Products | Two smaller nuclei + neutrons | One heavier nucleus |
| Conditions | Needs a neutron to start | Needs extreme heat & pressure |
| Where it occurs | Nuclear power stations | Stars (Sun) |
| Energy released | Large | Very large |
| Waste produced | Radioactive waste | Minimal radioactive waste |
| Controlled on Earth? | Yes (power stations) | Not yet practical |

KEY CONCEPTS:

THE ATOM (19.1):
• Structure: positive nucleus with orbiting negative electrons
• Most of atom is empty space
• Ions: atoms that have lost (positive ion) or gained (negative ion) electrons
• Rutherford's experiment: proved nuclear model via alpha scattering

THE NUCLEUS (19.2):
• Composed of protons (+1) and neutrons (0)
• Proton number (Z) = number of protons = atomic number
• Nucleon number (A) = protons + neutrons = mass number
• Neutrons = A − Z
• Nuclide notation: ᴬzX
• Isotopes: same element, same protons, different neutrons
• Nuclear charge = +Z; Nuclear mass ≈ A

FISSION AND FUSION (19.3):
• Fission: large nucleus splits into smaller nuclei + neutrons + energy
• Fusion: light nuclei join to form heavier nucleus + energy
• Both conserve nucleon number and proton number
• Mass is converted to energy in both processes

|----------|----------|-----------------|---------------|

7. Nucleon conservation: 2 + 2 = 3 + A → A = 1
   Proton conservation: 1 + 1 = 2 + Z → Z = 0
   The particle is a neutron: ¹₀n

================================================================================
                    COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS
================================================================================

| Misconception | Correct Understanding |
|---------------|----------------------|
| Electrons are in the nucleus | Electrons orbit the nucleus; only protons and neutrons are in the nucleus |
| Atoms can be seen with a microscope | Atoms are far too small; even electron microscopes show limits |
| The nucleus takes up most of the atom | The nucleus is tiny; most of the atom is empty space |
| Isotopes are different elements | Isotopes are the SAME element with different neutron numbers |
| Mass number = number of protons | Mass number = protons + neutrons |
| Fission and fusion are the same | Fission splits heavy nuclei; fusion joins light nuclei |
| Nuclear reactions create or destroy mass | Mass is converted to energy, not destroyed |
