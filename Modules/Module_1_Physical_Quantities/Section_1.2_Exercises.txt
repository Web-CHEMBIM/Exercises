SECTION J: ERROR ANALYSIS

49. (a) Velocity requires a direction; the student only stated a
        magnitude [B1].
    (b) "I walked 500 m north at a velocity of 1.5 m/s north"
        OR "I walked a distance of 500 m at a speed of 1.5 m/s" [B1].
    (c) If the student walked in a straight line north, 500 m is both
        the distance and the displacement [B1]. In this special case
        they are equal because the path is a straight line with no
        change of direction [B1].

50. (a) The student ignores that velocity (v) in p = mv is a vector,
        not a scalar [B1].
    (b) Momentum = mass × velocity. Since velocity is a vector (has
        direction), the product mv also has direction [B1]. Therefore
        momentum is a vector — it has both magnitude and direction [B1].
    (c) Weight [B1]. Weight = mg, where g is gravitational field
        strength (a vector). Weight always acts vertically downward,
        so it has a specific direction and is therefore a vector [B1].
