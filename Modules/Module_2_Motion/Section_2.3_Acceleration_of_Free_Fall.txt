2.3.1  ACCELERATION OF FREE FALL
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
INTRODUCTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

If you drop a hammer and a feather at the same time, which hits the ground first? 
On Earth, the hammer wins every time. But why? Is it because it's heavier?

In 1971, astronaut David Scott performed this experiment on the Moon. He dropped 
a hammer and a feather, and they hit the ground at exactly the same time! 
Use the link below to watch this famous experiment:
[search_web: Apollo 15 Hammer and Feather Drop]

This section explains why objects fall the way they do and introduces a special 
constant in physics: g.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WHAT IS FREE FALL?
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

> DEFINITION
> Free fall is motion where the only force acting on an object is gravity.

In true free fall, there is no air resistance (drag). This only happens perfectly 
in a vacuum.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ACCELERATION DUE TO GRAVITY (g)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

When an object falls freely near the Earth's surface, it accelerates downwards. 
This acceleration is caused by the Earth's gravitational pull.

KEY FACTS:
1. The acceleration is approximately constant near the Earth's surface.
2. The value is approximately 9.8 m/s².
3. It is denoted by the symbol 'g'.

> IMPORTANT VALUE
> g ≈ 9.8 m/s²

This means that for every second an object falls, its downward velocity increases 
by 9.8 m/s.

t = 0 s   ---> v = 0 m/s
t = 1 s   ---> v = 9.8 m/s
t = 2 s   ---> v = 19.6 m/s
t = 3 s   ---> v = 29.4 m/s

Does mass matter?
NO! In the absence of air resistance, all objects fall with the SAME acceleration, 
regardless of their mass. A heavy stone and a light pebble will accelerate at the 
same rate (9.8 m/s²) if air resistance is ignored.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WORKED EXAMPLE 1: Calculates with g
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PROBLEM:
A stone is dropped from a bridge. Ignoring air resistance, calculate its 
velocity after 3.0 seconds. (Take g = 9.8 m/s²)

SOLUTION:

Step 1: Identify given values
    u = 0 m/s (dropped from rest)
    a = g = 9.8 m/s²
    t = 3.0 s
    Required: v = ?

Step 2: Select equation
    v = u + at

Step 3: Substitute
    v = 0 + (9.8 × 3.0)

Step 4: Calculate
    v = 29.4 m/s

Step 5: State answer
    The stone's velocity is 29.4 m/s.

================================================================================
2.3.2  MOTION WITH AIR RESISTANCE
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
FALLING IN THE REAL WORLD
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

In everyday life, objects fall through air. The air exerts a resistive force 
called 'air resistance' or 'drag'.

Air resistance depends on:
1. Speed: The faster the object moves, the greater the air resistance.
2. Surface Area: The larger the surface area, the greater the air resistance.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE STAGES OF FALLING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Let's look at what happens to a skydiver jumping out of a plane.

STAGE 1: JUST RELEASED (t = 0)
- Velocity is zero.
- Air resistance is zero.
- Only force is weight (gravity) pulling down.
- Resultant force is downwards.
- Acceleration = g (9.8 m/s²).

STAGE 2: ACCELERATING
- Speed increases.
- Air resistance INCREASES as speed increases.
- Air resistance opposes weight (acts upwards).
- Resultant force downwards decreases (Weight - Air Resistance).
- Acceleration decreases (but is still downward, so speed actually still increases!).

STAGE 3: TERMINAL VELOCITY
- Speed has increased enough that Air Resistance = Weight.
- Resultant force is ZERO.
- Acceleration is ZERO.
- The object continues to fall at a CONSTANT speed.
- This constant speed is called TERMINAL VELOCITY.

> DEFINITION
> Terminal velocity is the constant maximum velocity reached by a falling object 
> when the resistive force (air resistance) equals the accelerating force (weight).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
GRAPHING THE MOTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

We can compare motion with and without air resistance using a speed-time graph.

WITHOUT AIR RESISTANCE (Vacuum):
- Straight line graph starting from origin.
- Gradient is constant (gradient = g).
- Speed increases indefinitely (until impact).

WITH AIR RESISTANCE:
- Curve starting from origin.
- Gradient is initially steep (gradient = g).
- Gradient decreases as time goes on (acceleration decreases).
- Graph becomes horizontal (acceleration = 0).
- The horizontal value represents terminal velocity.

================================================================================
SECTION 2.3 SUMMARY
================================================================================

KEY POINTS:
• Acceleration of free fall (g) is approx 9.8 m/s² near Earth's surface.
• This value is constant for all objects near Earth, regardless of mass.
• 'Free fall' means gravity is the only force acting (no air resistance).
• Air resistance (drag) increases with speed and opposes motion.
• Terminal velocity is reached when Air Resistance = Weight.
• At terminal velocity, acceleration is zero.
