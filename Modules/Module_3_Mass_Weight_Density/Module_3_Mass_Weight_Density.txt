--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.1.1  MASS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

INTRODUCTION
---------------
Have you ever wondered why it's harder to push a shopping trolley when it's full
compared to when it's empty? The answer lies in understanding mass - one of the
most fundamental concepts in physics.

> DEFINITION
> Mass is a measure of the quantity of matter in an object at rest relative to
> the observer.

KEY POINTS ABOUT MASS:

* Mass is a measure of how much "stuff" (matter) an object contains
* The greater the mass, the more difficult it is to change the object's motion
  (speed it up, slow it down, or change its direction)
* Mass is a SCALAR quantity - it has magnitude (size) only, with no direction
* Mass is measured in kilograms (kg)

SI UNITS:
┌─────────────────┬─────────────────┬─────────────────┐
│ Quantity        │ SI Unit         │ Symbol          │
├─────────────────┼─────────────────┼─────────────────┤
│ Mass            │ kilogram        │ kg              │
└─────────────────┴─────────────────┴─────────────────┘

UNIT CONVERSION:
Sometimes mass is given in grams (g), but calculations require kilograms (kg).

  1000 g = 1 kg
  1 g = 0.001 kg

  To convert g to kg: divide by 1000
  To convert kg to g: multiply by 1000


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WORKED EXAMPLE 1: Converting Mass Units
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PROBLEM:
An object has a mass of 4.5 kg. State the mass of the object in grams (g).

SOLUTION:

Step 1: State the conversion between g and kg
    1 kg = 1000 g

Step 2: Convert kg to g by multiplying
    m = 4.5 × 1000

Step 3: State the answer
    m = 4500 g


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.1.2  WEIGHT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

INTRODUCTION
---------------
When you hold a heavy book, you feel it pulling downward. This "pull" you feel
is not the book's mass - it's the book's weight! Weight and mass are different
quantities, though people often confuse them in everyday speech.

> DEFINITION
> Weight is a gravitational force on an object that has mass.

KEY POINTS ABOUT WEIGHT:

* Weight is a force - the gravitational pull of a planet on an object
* Weight is a VECTOR quantity - it has both magnitude and direction
* Weight always acts downward (toward the centre of the Earth)
* Weight is measured in newtons (N)

IMPORTANT DISTINCTION:
┌─────────────────┬─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ MASS            │ WEIGHT                                                      │
├─────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Amount of       │ Gravitational force on an object                            │
│ matter          │                                                             │
├─────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Scalar quantity │ Vector quantity                                             │
├─────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Measured in kg  │ Measured in N                                               │
├─────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Same everywhere │ Changes depending on gravitational field strength           │
│ in the Universe │                                                             │
└─────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

NOTE: An object's mass stays the same wherever it is in the Universe.
However, its weight changes depending on the strength of gravity at that
location. On the Moon, you would weigh about 1/6 of your weight on Earth, but
your mass would be exactly the same!

3.1.3  GRAVITATIONAL FIELD STRENGTH
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

(Extended Tier)

INTRODUCTION
---------------
Why do objects fall to the ground when released? Because Earth has a
gravitational field that attracts all objects with mass. The strength of this
field determines how strongly objects are pulled downward.

> DEFINITION
> Gravitational field strength is the force per unit mass acting on an object
> in a gravitational field.

FORMULA:

                          W
                g = ─────────
                          m

where:
    g = gravitational field strength (in N/kg)
    W = weight (in N)
    m = mass (in kg)

On Earth, g ≈ 9.8 N/kg

The formula can be rearranged to find weight:

    W = m × g

IMPORTANT:
Gravitational field strength (g) has the same numerical value as the
acceleration of free fall. This is why we also write g = 9.8 m/s².

COMPARING GRAVITATIONAL FIELD STRENGTH:
┌─────────────────┬─────────────────┐
│ Location        │ g (N/kg)        │
├─────────────────┼─────────────────┤
│ Earth           │ 9.8             │
├─────────────────┼─────────────────┤
│ Moon            │ 1.6             │
├─────────────────┼─────────────────┤
│ Mars            │ 3.7             │
├─────────────────┼─────────────────┤
│ Jupiter         │ 24.8            │
└─────────────────┴─────────────────┘

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WORKED EXAMPLE 2: Calculating Weight on Earth and Moon
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PROBLEM:
Isabelle has a mass of 42 kg. Compare the difference between Isabelle's weight
on Earth and her weight on the Moon.
Take the Earth's gravitational field strength as 9.8 N/kg, and the Moon's
gravitational field strength as 1.6 N/kg.

SOLUTION:

Step 1: List the known values
    Mass, m = 42 kg
    Gravitational field strength on Earth, g_E = 9.8 N/kg
    Gravitational field strength on Moon, g_M = 1.6 N/kg

Step 2: State the equation
    W = m × g

Step 3: Calculate the weight on Earth
    W_E = m × g_E
    W_E = 42 × 9.8
    W_E = 411.6 N ≈ 410 N (2 s.f.)

Step 4: Calculate the weight on the Moon
    W_M = m × g_M
    W_M = 42 × 1.6
    W_M = 67.2 N ≈ 67 N (2 s.f.)

Step 5: Compare the two values
    Isabelle's weight on Earth (410 N) is much greater than on the Moon (67 N).
    This is because Earth has a larger gravitational field strength than the
    Moon. Her mass remains the same (42 kg) in both locations.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
USING A BALANCE TO COMPARE MASSES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Masses (and weights) can be compared using a balance.

HOW IT WORKS:
* A balance compares the weight of two objects
* Since gravitational field strength is constant at any one location, comparing
  weights also compares masses
* We can use the equation: m = W/g

TYPES OF BALANCES:
1. Digital balance - gives mass reading directly in kg or g
2. Beam balance - compares unknown mass with known masses

FORCE METERS:
* Also called newton meters or spring balances
* Measure weight directly in newtons (N)
* The object is hung from a hook and stretches a spring

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.2.1  DEFINITION AND EQUATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

INTRODUCTION
---------------
Why does a bag of feathers feel lighter than a same-sized bag of metal? Why
does a balloon float but a small lead weight sinks? The answer lies in density
- how much mass is packed into a given volume.

> DEFINITION
> Density is the mass per unit volume of a material.

FORMULA:

                          m
                ρ = ─────────
                          V

where:
    ρ = density (in kg/m³ or g/cm³)
    m = mass (in kg or g)
    V = volume (in m³ or cm³)

This formula can be rearranged:
    m = ρ × V     (to find mass)
    V = m / ρ     (to find volume)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WORKED EXAMPLE 3: Calculating Density
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PROBLEM:
A paving slab has a mass of 73 kg and dimensions 0.04 m × 0.5 m × 0.85 m.
Calculate the density, in kg/m³, of the material from which the paving slab
is made.

SOLUTION:

Step 1: List the known quantities
    Mass of slab, m = 73 kg
    Dimensions = 0.04 m × 0.5 m × 0.85 m

Step 2: Calculate the volume
    V = 0.04 × 0.5 × 0.85
    V = 0.017 m³

Step 3: Write out the equation for density
    ρ = m / V

Step 4: Substitute in values
    ρ = 73 / 0.017

Step 5: Calculate
    ρ = 4294 kg/m³

Step 6: Round to appropriate significant figures
    ρ ≈ 4300 kg/m³


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.2.2  UNDERSTANDING DENSITY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

COMPARING DENSITIES:

* Objects made from low-density materials typically have a low mass
* Similarly-sized objects made from high-density materials have a high mass

EXAMPLE:
A bag full of feathers is much lighter than the same bag full of metal, even
though they occupy the same volume. This is because metal has a much higher
density than feathers.

COMPARING STATES OF MATTER:
┌─────────────────┬─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ State           │ Density Characteristics                                     │
├─────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Solids          │ Usually highest density - particles tightly packed          │
├─────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Liquids         │ Usually medium density - particles close but can move       │
├─────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Gases           │ Lowest density - particles spread out with large gaps       │
└─────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

NOTE: A gas is less dense than the same substance in liquid or solid form
because the particles are more spread out (same mass over a larger volume).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.3.1  MEASURING DENSITY OF REGULAR OBJECTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

INTRODUCTION
---------------
How do we find the density of a solid object? We need to measure its mass and
volume, then use the formula ρ = m/V. For regularly shaped objects (cubes,
rectangular blocks, cylinders), we can calculate the volume from measurements
of their dimensions.

EQUIPMENT:
┌─────────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Apparatus                   │ Purpose                                         │
├─────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Digital balance             │ To measure the mass of the object               │
├─────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 30 cm ruler                 │ To measure objects up to 30 cm (resolution 1mm) │
├─────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Vernier calipers            │ For more precise measurements (resolution 0.1mm)│
├─────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Micrometer                  │ For very precise measurements (resolution 0.01mm│
└─────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

METHOD:

1. Place the object on a digital balance and record its mass

2. Use a ruler (or vernier calipers or micrometer depending on size) to measure
   the object's dimensions (length, width, height, or radius)

3. Repeat these measurements and take an average to reduce random errors

4. Calculate the volume using the appropriate formula

5. Calculate the density using ρ = m/V

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.3.2  MEASURING DENSITY OF IRREGULAR OBJECTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

INTRODUCTION
---------------
What if the object is an irregular shape, like a stone or a key? We can't
easily calculate its volume from dimensions. Instead, we use the displacement
method - the object displaces its own volume of water when submerged.

EQUIPMENT:
┌─────────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Apparatus                   │ Purpose                                         │
├─────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Digital balance             │ To measure the mass of the object               │
├─────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Eureka can (displacement can│ To measure volume by displacement               │
├─────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Measuring cylinder          │ To collect and measure displaced water          │
└─────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

METHOD (EUREKA CAN):

1. Place the object on a digital balance and record its mass

2. Fill the Eureka can with water up to just below the spout

3. Place an empty measuring cylinder below the spout

4. Carefully lower the object into the Eureka can

5. Water will flow out through the spout into the measuring cylinder

6. Read the volume of displaced water - this equals the volume of the object

7. Calculate the density using ρ = m/V

ALTERNATIVE METHOD (MEASURING CYLINDER):

1. Measure the mass of the object on a balance

2. Fill a measuring cylinder with a known volume of water (V₁)

3. Carefully lower the object into the water

4. Read the new water level (V₂)

5. Volume of object = V₂ - V₁

6. Calculate the density using ρ = m/V

WORKED EXAMPLE 4: Finding Density by Displacement
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PROBLEM:
A student measures the density of an irregular stone. The mass is 45 g. When
placed in a measuring cylinder containing 50 cm³ of water, the water level
rises to 65 cm³. Calculate the density of the stone.

SOLUTION:

Step 1: List the known values
    Mass, m = 45 g
    Initial water level, V₁ = 50 cm³
    Final water level, V₂ = 65 cm³

Step 2: Calculate the volume of the stone
    V = V₂ - V₁
    V = 65 - 50
    V = 15 cm³

Step 3: Calculate the density
    ρ = m / V
    ρ = 45 / 15
    ρ = 3.0 g/cm³

3.3.3  MEASURING DENSITY OF LIQUIDS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

METHOD:

1. Place an empty measuring cylinder on a digital balance

2. Reset the balance to zero (or note the mass of the empty cylinder)

3. Pour the liquid into the measuring cylinder and note the volume

4. Read the new mass on the balance

5. Calculate the mass of liquid = final mass - mass of empty cylinder

6. Calculate the density using ρ = m/V

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.4.1  DENSITY AND FLOATING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

INTRODUCTION
---------------
Have you ever wondered why a heavy steel ship floats while a small stone
sinks? Or why some objects float on water but others don't? The answer depends
on comparing the densities of the object and the fluid.

> KEY PRINCIPLE
> Whether an object floats or sinks depends on the relative densities of the
> object and the fluid it is placed in.

FLOATING AND SINKING RULES:
┌─────────────────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Condition                           │ Result                                │
├─────────────────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Object density > Fluid density      │ Object SINKS                          │
├─────────────────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Object density < Fluid density      │ Object FLOATS                         │
├─────────────────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Object density = Fluid density      │ Object stays at current position      │
│                                     │ (neither floats nor sinks)            │
└─────────────────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────────────┘

UPTHRUST:

When an object is placed in a fluid:
* The fluid exerts an upward force called UPTHRUST
* Upthrust acts in the opposite direction to weight
* The size of upthrust depends on:
  - The density of the fluid
  - The volume of fluid displaced (equal to the volume of the submerged object)

An object floats when upthrust = weight

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WORKED EXAMPLE 5: Determining Whether an Object Floats
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PROBLEM:
Potassium is an alkali metal with a density of 0.862 g/cm³. A science teacher
has four different mineral oils in which they could store their potassium.
Which mineral oil should the teacher choose if the potassium must sink?

A: White mineral oil, ρ = 0.864 g/cm³
B: Paraffin oil, ρ = 0.890 g/cm³
C: Liquid paraffin, ρ = 0.825 g/cm³
D: Food grade mineral oil, ρ = 0.880 g/cm³

SOLUTION:

Step 1: State the condition for sinking
    An object sinks if its density is greater than the fluid's density.
    For potassium to sink, the oil must have a density < 0.862 g/cm³

Step 2: Compare the densities
    A: 0.864 g/cm³ > 0.862 g/cm³ → potassium would float
    B: 0.890 g/cm³ > 0.862 g/cm³ → potassium would float
    C: 0.825 g/cm³ < 0.862 g/cm³ → potassium would SINK ✓
    D: 0.880 g/cm³ > 0.862 g/cm³ → potassium would float

Step 3: State the answer
    The teacher should choose C: Liquid paraffin.
    This is the only oil with a density less than potassium, so the potassium
    will sink.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.4.2  FLOATING LIQUIDS (Extended)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

(Extended Tier)

When two liquids that do not mix are placed together:

> KEY PRINCIPLE
> A liquid with a lower density will float on top of a liquid with a higher
> density, provided the liquids do not mix.

EXAMPLE: Oil and Water
* Oil has a density of about 0.8 - 0.9 g/cm³
* Water has a density of 1.0 g/cm³
* Oil floats on top of water because it is less dense
* They do not mix because oil and water are immiscible


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WORKED EXAMPLE 6: Floating Liquids
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PROBLEM:
Liquid A has a density of 0.76 g/cm³ and liquid B has a density of 0.93 g/cm³.
If the two liquids do not mix, which liquid will float on top of the other?

SOLUTION:

Step 1: List the known quantities
    Liquid A = 0.76 g/cm³
    Liquid B = 0.93 g/cm³

Step 2: Compare the densities
    0.76 is less than 0.93
    Therefore, Liquid A has the lower density.

Step 3: Apply the rule
    The liquid with lower density floats on top.

Step 4: State the answer
    Liquid A will float on top of Liquid B.

* Gravitational field strength on Earth, g = 9.8 N/kg (or m/s²)
* Density of water = 1.0 g/cm³ = 1000 kg/m³

* Mass is constant everywhere; weight depends on gravitational field strength
* Objects float when their density is less than the fluid's density
* Volume of irregular objects can be found using displacement

SECTION 3.1: Mass and Weight
----------------------------

Q1. Mass is a measure of the quantity of matter in an object at rest relative
    to the observer.

Q2. Two differences:
    - Mass is a scalar quantity; weight is a vector quantity
    - Mass is measured in kg; weight is measured in N
    (Also acceptable: mass is constant everywhere; weight depends on location)

Q3. W = m × g = 50 × 9.8 = 490 N

Q4. m = W / g = 784 / 9.8 = 80 kg

Q5. The astronaut's mass stays the same because mass is the amount of matter,
    which doesn't change with location. However, weight changes because the
    Moon has a weaker gravitational field strength than Earth. Since W = mg
    and g is smaller on the Moon, the weight is smaller.


SECTION 3.2: Density
----------------------------

Q6. Density is the mass per unit volume of a material.

Q7. ρ = m / V

Q8. Volume = 0.02 × 0.02 × 0.02 = 0.000008 m³ = 8 × 10⁻⁶ m³
    Mass = 64 g = 0.064 kg
    Density = 0.064 / (8 × 10⁻⁶) = 8000 kg/m³

Q9. m = ρ × V = 0.8 × 500 = 400 g

Q10. Ice has a density of about 0.92 g/cm³, which is less than water's density
     of 1.0 g/cm³. Objects less dense than water will float.


SECTION 3.3: Measuring Density
----------------------------

Q11. 1. Measure the mass of the block using a digital balance
     2. Measure the length, width and height using a ruler
     3. Calculate volume: V = l × w × h
     4. Calculate density: ρ = m / V

Q12. The displacement method is used because irregular objects have complex
     shapes that cannot be easily measured with a ruler. When submerged in
     water, the volume of water displaced equals the volume of the object.

Q13. V = 80 - 40 = 40 cm³
     ρ = m / V = 120 / 40 = 3.0 g/cm³

Q14. - Zero the balance before measuring
     - Read the measuring cylinder at eye level
     - Take repeat readings and calculate an average
     - Handle glassware carefully


SECTION 3.4: Floating and Sinking
----------------------------

Q15. An object floats when its density is less than the density of the liquid.
     (Or: when the upthrust force equals its weight)

Q16. It will float. 0.85 g/cm³ < 1.0 g/cm³, so the block is less dense than
     water. Objects less dense than the surrounding fluid will float.

Q17. From top to bottom: Q (0.9 g/cm³), P (1.2 g/cm³), R (1.5 g/cm³)
     The least dense liquid floats on top, the most dense sinks to the bottom.
