================================================================================
          SECTION 3.3 MEASURING DENSITY - PRACTICE EXERCISES
================================================================================

Learning Objectives Covered:
  * Describe how to determine the density of a liquid.
  * Describe how to determine the density of a regularly shaped solid.
  * Describe how to determine the density of an irregularly shaped solid
    which sinks in a liquid (volume by displacement).

================================================================================
                    SECTION A: MULTIPLE CHOICE
================================================================================

1. To find the density of a regular solid, you need to measure:
   A: Mass and time
   B: Mass and volume
   C: Volume and speed
   D: Force and area

2. The volume of an irregularly shaped stone can be found using:
   A: A ruler
   B: A stopwatch
   C: The displacement method
   D: A balance

3. A metal block has a mass of 540 g and a volume of 200 cm³.
   What is its density?
   A: 0.37 g/cm³
   B: 2.7 g/cm³
   C: 340 g/cm³
   D: 108 000 g/cm³

4. A measuring cylinder contains 40 cm³ of water. When a stone is
   added, the level rises to 55 cm³. The volume of the stone is:
   A: 15 cm³
   B: 40 cm³
   C: 55 cm³
   D: 95 cm³

5. To measure the density of a liquid, you need:
   A: A ruler and a balance
   B: A measuring cylinder and a balance
   C: A thermometer and a stopwatch
   D: A force meter and a ruler

6. A Eureka can is used to measure:
   A: The mass of an object
   B: The temperature of water
   C: The volume of an irregular object by displacement
   D: The weight of an object

7. Which instrument is best for measuring the dimensions of a small
   metal sphere for a density calculation?
   A: Metre ruler
   B: Tape measure
   C: Vernier calipers
   D: Trundle wheel

8. A student measures the density of a liquid as 0.8 g/cm³.
   What is this in kg/m³?
   A: 0.0008 kg/m³
   B: 0.8 kg/m³
   C: 80 kg/m³
   D: 800 kg/m³

================================================================================
                    SECTION B: SHORT ANSWER & STRUCTURED
================================================================================

9. Describe the step-by-step method for finding the density of a
   rectangular metal block. Include the measurements needed and
   the calculations. [4]

10. A student wants to find the density of an irregularly shaped stone.
    Describe the displacement method she should use. [4]

11. An aluminium cube has sides of length 3 cm and a mass of 72.9 g.

    (a) Calculate the volume of the cube in cm³. [1]

    (b) Calculate the density of aluminium. [2]

    (c) Convert your answer to kg/m³. [1]

12. A student measures the density of a liquid using a measuring
    cylinder and a balance.

    Her results are:
    - Mass of empty measuring cylinder = 85 g
    - Mass of cylinder + liquid = 165 g
    - Volume of liquid in cylinder = 100 cm³

    (a) Calculate the mass of the liquid. [1]

    (b) Calculate the density of the liquid. [2]

    (c) The student suspects the liquid is cooking oil (density 0.92 g/cm³).
        Does her result support this? Explain your answer. [2]

13. A student uses a Eureka can to find the volume of a small rock.

    The mass of the rock is 84 g.
    The water displaced by the rock fills a measuring cylinder
    to the 28 cm³ mark.

    (a) State the volume of the rock. [1]

    (b) Calculate the density of the rock. [2]

    (c) Water has a density of 1.0 g/cm³. Explain why the rock sinks
        in water, using your calculated density. [1]

14. A student is given two metal cylinders, A and B, that look identical
    in size. She is asked to determine whether they are made of the
    same material.

    Her measurements:
    ┌──────────┬───────────────┬───────────────┬───────────────┐
    │ Cylinder │ Mass (g)      │ Diameter (cm) │ Height (cm)   │
    ├──────────┼───────────────┼───────────────┼───────────────┤
    │ A        │ 169.6         │ 2.0           │ 5.0           │
    ├──────────┼───────────────┼───────────────┼───────────────┤
    │ B        │ 124.4         │ 2.0           │ 5.0           │
    └──────────┴───────────────┴───────────────┴───────────────┘

    (a) Calculate the volume of each cylinder.
        (V = πr²h; use π = 3.14) [2]

    (b) Calculate the density of cylinder A. [2]

    (c) Calculate the density of cylinder B. [2]

    (d) Are the cylinders made of the same material? Justify your
        answer. [2]

================================================================================
                    ANSWER KEY
================================================================================

1.  B (Mass and volume) - ρ = m/V
2.  C (The displacement method) - submerge in water
3.  B (2.7 g/cm³) - ρ = 540/200 = 2.7 g/cm³
4.  A (15 cm³) - V = 55 − 40 = 15 cm³
5.  B (A measuring cylinder and a balance)
6.  C (The volume of an irregular object by displacement)
7.  C (Vernier calipers) - higher resolution for small objects
8.  D (800 kg/m³) - multiply by 1000 to convert g/cm³ to kg/m³

9.  1) Place the block on a digital balance and record its mass [1].
    2) Use a ruler or vernier calipers to measure the length, width
       and height of the block [1].
    3) Calculate the volume: V = l × w × h [1].
    4) Calculate the density: ρ = m / V [1].

10. 1) Measure the mass of the stone on a digital balance [1].
    2) Fill a measuring cylinder with water and record the level (V₁) [1].
    3) Carefully lower the stone into the water and record the new
       level (V₂) [1].
    4) Volume of stone = V₂ − V₁. Calculate density: ρ = m / V [1].
    (Alternatively, describe using a Eureka can.)

11. (a) V = 3 × 3 × 3 = 27 cm³ [1]
    (b) ρ = m / V = 72.9 / 27 [1] = 2.7 g/cm³ [1]
    (c) 2.7 × 1000 = 2700 kg/m³ [1]

12. (a) Mass of liquid = 165 − 85 = 80 g [1]
    (b) ρ = m / V = 80 / 100 [1] = 0.80 g/cm³ [1]
    (c) The measured density (0.80 g/cm³) is close to but slightly
        lower than cooking oil (0.92 g/cm³) [1]. The result does not
        strongly support this — there is a difference of about 13%,
        which is beyond normal experimental error. The liquid may be
        a different type of oil or another substance [1].

13. (a) Volume of rock = 28 cm³ [1]
    (b) ρ = m / V = 84 / 28 [1] = 3.0 g/cm³ [1]
    (c) The rock's density (3.0 g/cm³) is greater than water's density
        (1.0 g/cm³), so the rock sinks [1].

14. (a) r = 2.0 / 2 = 1.0 cm
        V = πr²h = 3.14 × 1.0² × 5.0 [1] = 15.7 cm³ [1]
        (Both cylinders have the same volume.)
    (b) ρ_A = 169.6 / 15.7 [1] = 10.8 g/cm³ [1]
    (c) ρ_B = 124.4 / 15.7 [1] = 7.9 g/cm³ [1]
    (d) No, they are not made of the same material [1]. They have
        different densities (10.8 g/cm³ vs 7.9 g/cm³). Cylinder A
        could be silver or lead; Cylinder B could be iron/steel [1].
