--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.1.1  EFFECTS OF FORCES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

INTRODUCTION
---------------
Think about pushing a shopping trolley, kicking a football, or squeezing a
stress ball. In each case, you are applying a force. But what exactly is a
force, and what does it do?

> DEFINITION
> A force is a push or a pull that acts on an object due to the interaction
> with another object.

Forces can have a variety of effects on an object. Forces can change an
object's:
  * Speed      - making it go faster or slower
  * Direction  - changing which way it moves
  * Shape      - stretching, compressing or bending it
  * Size       - making it larger or smaller

EXAMPLES OF FORCE EFFECTS:
┌────────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Force Applied              │ Effect                                          │
├────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Engine thrust on a car     │ Causes the car to speed up                      │
├────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Gravitational pull on      │ Causes the comet to change direction            │
│ a comet                    │                                                 │
├────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Compression forces on      │ Causes the spring to change shape               │
│ a spring                   │                                                 │
├────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Braking force (friction)   │ Causes the car to slow down                     │
└────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

NOTE: Force is a VECTOR quantity - it has both magnitude (size) and direction.
Force is measured in newtons (N).


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.1.2  RESULTANT FORCES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

WHAT IS A RESULTANT FORCE?
---------------------------

When multiple forces act on an object, we can combine them to find a single
force that describes the combined effect of all forces.

> DEFINITION
> A resultant force is a single force that describes all of the forces
> operating on a body.

The resultant force determines:
  - The direction in which the object will move
  - The magnitude of the net force experienced by the object


BALANCED AND UNBALANCED FORCES
-------------------------------

Forces acting on an object can be described as balanced or unbalanced:

┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ BALANCED FORCES                                                             │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ • Forces act in opposing directions with equal magnitude                    │
│ • The effects of the forces cancel out                                      │
│ • There is NO resultant force (resultant = 0)                               │
│ • The object remains at rest OR continues at constant velocity              │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ UNBALANCED FORCES                                                           │
├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ • Forces do not cancel out                                                  │
│ • There IS a resultant force                                                │
│ • The object will accelerate (change velocity)                              │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

EXAMPLE: A Book on a Table

Consider a book resting on a table:
  - The gravitational pull of the Earth on the book (weight) acts DOWNWARD
  - The push force of the table on the book (normal contact force) acts UPWARD
  - These forces are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction
  - The forces are BALANCED
  - There is no resultant force acting on the book
  - The book remains at rest

  - The forces are UNBALANCED
  - There is a resultant force acting upwards
  - The rocket accelerates upwards

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.1.3  CALCULATING RESULTANT FORCE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Since force is a vector quantity, when adding forces together we must consider
their direction.

SIGN CONVENTION:
  - Choose one direction as positive (e.g., right or up)
  - The opposite direction is negative (e.g., left or down)
  - Add all forces together, keeping their signs


EXAMPLE 1: Forces in the Same Direction

If two people push a box from the same side:
  - Person 1 pushes with 3 N to the right (+3 N)
  - Person 2 pushes with 7 N to the right (+7 N)

  Resultant force = (+3) + (+7) = +10 N to the right

The box will move to the right with a force of 10 N.


EXAMPLE 2: Forces in Opposite Directions

If two people push a box from opposite sides:
  - Person 1 pushes with 7 N to the left (-7 N)
  - Person 2 pushes with 3 N to the right (+3 N)

  Resultant force = (-7) + (+3) = -4 N

The box will move to the LEFT with a force of 4 N.


EXAMPLE 3: Equal Opposite Forces

If two people push with equal force from opposite sides:
  - Person 1 pushes with 5 N to the left (-5 N)
  - Person 2 pushes with 5 N to the right (+5 N)

  Resultant force = (-5) + (+5) = 0 N

The forces are balanced; the box doesn't move.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WORKED EXAMPLE 1: Calculating Resultant Force
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PROBLEM:
Three forces act on an object along a straight line:
  - 14 N to the left
  - 4 N to the right
  - 8 N to the right

Calculate the magnitude and direction of the resultant force.

SOLUTION:

Step 1: Assign a direction to the forces
    Forces acting to the right are positive (+)
    Forces acting to the left are negative (-)

Step 2: Convert forces with their signs
    14 N to the left = -14 N
    4 N to the right = +4 N
    8 N to the right = +8 N

Step 3: Add together all the forces
    Resultant force = (-14) + (+4) + (+8)
    Resultant force = -14 + 12
    Resultant force = -2 N

Step 4: State the magnitude and direction
    The resultant force is 2 N to the left.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.2.1  NEWTON'S FIRST LAW OF MOTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

INTRODUCTION
---------------
Why does a football keep rolling after you kick it? Why does a car need to
use fuel to keep moving? Isaac Newton answered these questions over 300 years
ago with his laws of motion.

> DEFINITION
> Newton's first law of motion states:
> Objects will remain at rest, or move with a constant velocity, unless acted
> on by a resultant force.

This means:
  - A stationary object will remain stationary
  - An object moving with constant velocity will continue at that velocity
  - UNLESS a resultant force acts on it

IMPORTANT POINTS:

1. Constant velocity means:
   - No change in speed
   - No change in direction

2. If an object is at rest OR moving at constant velocity, then:
   - No resultant force is acting on it
   - The forces are balanced

3. To change an object's velocity (speed up, slow down, or change direction):
   - A resultant force MUST act on the object

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WORKED EXAMPLE 2: Moon's Orbit
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PROBLEM:
A student did some research and found that the Moon orbits the Earth at a
constant speed of around 2000 mph.

The student says that this is NOT an example of Newton's first law of motion.
Is the student correct? Explain your answer.

SOLUTION:

Step 1: Recall Newton's first law of motion
    Newton's first law states that objects will remain at rest, or move with
    a constant VELOCITY, unless acted on by a resultant force.

Step 2: Determine if the Moon is moving with constant velocity
    The Moon is moving at a constant SPEED.
    However, the Moon is continually changing DIRECTION as it orbits the Earth.
    Therefore, its VELOCITY is not constant (velocity = speed + direction).

Step 3: State and explain the conclusion
    The student is CORRECT.
    The Moon moves with constant speed but always changes direction.
    Since velocity includes direction, the Moon's velocity is always changing.
    This means a resultant force IS acting on the Moon (gravitational pull).
    Therefore, this is NOT an example of Newton's first law.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WORKED EXAMPLE 3: Car at Constant Velocity
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PROBLEM:
A car moves at a constant velocity. The driving force from the engine is 3 kN.
Determine the frictional force acting on the car.

SOLUTION:

Step 1: Recall Newton's first law of motion
    Newton's first law states that objects moving at constant velocity have
    no resultant force acting on them.

Step 2: Relate Newton's first law to the scenario
    Since the car is moving at constant velocity:
    - There is no resultant force
    - The forces are balanced
    - Driving force = Frictional force

Step 3: State the value of the frictional force
    Frictional force = Driving force = 3 kN

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ MISCONCEPTION                  │ CORRECTION                                 │
├────────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ "An object needs a force to    │ An object moving in a straight line at    │
│ keep moving"                   │ constant speed will continue forever      │
│                                │ without any force (if there is no         │
│                                │ friction). Force is only needed to        │
│                                │ CHANGE its motion.                        │
├────────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ "If an object is moving,       │ An object moving at constant velocity     │

│                                │ balanced.                                 │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

EXAMINER TIP:
On Earth, there are always frictional forces acting on moving objects. A car
only maintains constant velocity when the engine force BALANCES the friction.
If the engine force increases, the car speeds up. If friction increases (or
engine force decreases), the car slows down.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.3.1  NEWTON'S SECOND LAW OF MOTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

INTRODUCTION
---------------
Newton's first law tells us what happens when forces are balanced. Newton's
second law tells us what happens when forces are UNBALANCED - when there is
a resultant force.

> DEFINITION
> Newton's second law of motion states:
> The acceleration of an object is proportional to the resultant force acting
> on it and inversely proportional to the object's mass.

This means:
  - A resultant force causes an ACCELERATION (change in velocity)
  - The bigger the force, the larger the acceleration
  - The bigger the mass, the smaller the acceleration

CHANGE IN MOTION:
A resultant force acting on an object will cause a change in motion:
  - Speeding up
  - Slowing down
  - Changing direction

The acceleration occurs in the SAME DIRECTION as the resultant force.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.3.2  THE EQUATION F = ma
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Newton's second law can be expressed as an equation:

                F = m × a

Where:
    F = resultant force, measured in newtons (N)
    m = mass of the object, measured in kilograms (kg)
    a = acceleration of the object, measured in metres per second squared (m/s²)


FORMULA TRIANGLE:

                     ┌───────┐
                     │   F   │
                     ├───┬───┤
                     │ m │ a │
                     └───┴───┘

To find any quantity, cover it and see how the others combine:
  - F = m × a (multiply)
  - m = F ÷ a (divide)
  - a = F ÷ m (divide)


UNDERSTANDING THE RELATIONSHIPS:

1. Force and Acceleration (at constant mass):
   - Doubling the force → doubles the acceleration
   - Halving the force → halves the acceleration
   - They are DIRECTLY PROPORTIONAL

2. Mass and Acceleration (at constant force):
   - Doubling the mass → halves the acceleration
   - Halving the mass → doubles the acceleration
   - They are INVERSELY PROPORTIONAL

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WORKED EXAMPLE 4: Force Required for Acceleration
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PROBLEM:
A car has a mass of 900 kg and can accelerate from 0 to 27 m/s in 3 seconds.
Calculate:
a) the acceleration of the car in the first 3 seconds
b) the force required to produce this acceleration

SOLUTION:

Part (a): Calculate acceleration

Step 1: List the known quantities
    Initial velocity, u = 0 m/s
    Final velocity, v = 27 m/s
    Time, t = 3 s

Step 2: State the equation for acceleration
              v - u
    a = ─────────────
               t

Step 3: Substitute the values
              27 - 0
    a = ─────────────
               3

Step 4: Calculate
    a = 9 m/s²


Part (b): Calculate force

Step 1: List the known quantities
    Mass of the car, m = 900 kg
    Acceleration, a = 9 m/s²

Step 2: Identify the equation to use
    F = ma

Step 3: Calculate the force
    F = 900 × 9
    F = 8100 N


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
WORKED EXAMPLE 5: Comparing Accelerations
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PROBLEM:
Three shopping trolleys A, B and C are being pushed using the same force.
Trolley A has mass 10 kg, B has mass 15 kg, and C has mass 20 kg.

State which trolley would have the smallest acceleration. Explain your answer.

SOLUTION:

Step 1: Identify which law of motion to apply
    The question involves force, mass and acceleration.
    Newton's second law applies: F = ma

Step 2: Rearrange the equation to make acceleration the subject
           F
    a = ─────
           m

Step 3: Explain the inverse proportionality
    Acceleration is INVERSELY PROPORTIONAL to mass.
    This means: for the same force, a larger mass will experience a
    smaller acceleration.

Step 4: State the answer
    Trolley C will have the smallest acceleration because it has the
    largest mass (20 kg).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

├─────────────────────────────┼───────────────────────────────────────────────┤

└─────────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────────────────────────┘

RESOLUTION OF MEASURING EQUIPMENT:
  - Ruler = 1 mm (0.1 cm)


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.4.3  METHOD
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

STEP-BY-STEP PROCEDURE:

1. Set up the apparatus with the spring hanging from the clamp stand.
   Fix the ruler to the clamp stand to reduce movement.

2. Attach a pointer to the bottom of the spring to help read the ruler.

3. Record the initial length of the spring (with no mass added) by reading
   the position of the pointer on the ruler.

4. Add the 100 g mass hanger to the spring.

5. Record the mass (in kg) and the new position on the ruler.

6. Add another 100 g mass to the hanger.

7. Record the new mass and position on the ruler.

8. Repeat this process, adding 100 g each time, until all masses have
   been added.

9. Remove the masses one at a time and repeat the entire process two more
   times to get three sets of readings.

10. Calculate the average length for each mass.

11. Calculate the extension for each mass:
    Extension = Final length − Original length

CALCULATING FORCE:
The force applied to the spring is the weight of the mass:

    W = m × g

Where:
    W = weight (force), measured in newtons (N)
    m = mass, measured in kilograms (kg)
    g = gravitational field strength = 9.8 N/kg

Example: For m = 0.1 kg:
    W = 0.1 × 9.8 = 0.98 N


CALCULATING EXTENSION:

    Extension = Final length − Original length

The original length is the length with no mass added.


PLOTTING THE GRAPH:

1. Plot force (F) on the y-axis and extension (x) on the x-axis
   (This gives a force-extension graph)

2. Draw a line of best fit through the points

3. If the graph is a straight line through the origin:
   - Force is DIRECTLY PROPORTIONAL to extension
   - This relationship is known as Hooke's Law

4.4.5  ERRORS AND SAFETY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

SYSTEMATIC ERRORS:
  - Ensure measurements on the ruler are taken at EYE LEVEL to avoid
    parallax error
  - Check that the ruler is vertical and fixed in position

RANDOM ERRORS:
  - Use a pointer at the bottom of the spring to improve precision
  - Wait a few seconds for the mass to become stationary before reading
  - Take repeat readings and calculate an average
  - Check the spring has not passed its limit of proportionality

SAFETY CONSIDERATIONS:
  - Wear goggles in case the spring snaps
  - Stand up during the experiment with no feet under the masses
  - Place a mat or soft material below the masses to prevent damage if dropped
  - Use a G-clamp to secure the clamp stand to the desk
  - Add masses carefully; do not pull the spring too hard

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.5.1  HOOKE'S LAW
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

> DEFINITION
> Hooke's Law states:
> The extension of an elastic object is directly proportional to the force
> applied, up to the limit of proportionality.

What does "directly proportional" mean?
  - If the force is doubled, the extension doubles
  - If the force is halved, the extension halves
  - The ratio F/x stays constant

The relationship can be written as an equation:

                F = k × x

Where:
    F = force applied, measured in newtons (N)
    k = spring constant, measured in newtons per metre (N/m)
    x = extension of spring, measured in metres (m)


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.5.2  SPRING CONSTANT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

> DEFINITION
> The spring constant is the force per unit extension.

The spring constant (k) tells us how stiff a spring is:
  - STIFF springs have a HIGH spring constant (hard to stretch)
  - STRETCHY springs have a LOW spring constant (easy to stretch)

Units: newtons per metre (N/m)

To calculate the spring constant:

              F
    k = ─────────
              x

NOTE: The spring constant can apply to any elastic object that obeys Hooke's
law, not just springs.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.5.3  THE LIMIT OF PROPORTIONALITY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

> DEFINITION
> The limit of proportionality is the point beyond which force and extension
> are no longer directly proportional.

Before the limit:
  - Force and extension are directly proportional
  - The graph is a straight line through the origin
  - Hooke's Law applies

After the limit:
  - Force and extension are NOT proportional
  - The graph curves
  - Hooke's Law no longer applies
  - The spring may be permanently deformed

WORKED EXAMPLE 6: Calculating Weight Using Hooke's Law
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PROBLEM:
A child balances on a pogo stick. The spring constant of the spring is 
4900 N/m. The child's weight causes the spring to compress from 40 cm to 33 cm.
Calculate the weight of the child.

SOLUTION:

Step 1: List the known quantities
    Spring constant, k = 4900 N/m
    Original length = 40 cm
    Final length = 33 cm

Step 2: Write the relevant equation
    F = kx

Step 3: Calculate the compression (extension), x
    x = final length − original length
    x = 33 − 40 = −7 cm

    (A negative value indicates compression rather than extension)

Step 4: Convert to SI units (cm to m)
    x = 7 ÷ 100 = 0.07 m

Step 5: Substitute into the equation
    F = k × x
    F = 4900 × 0.07
    F = 343 N

Step 6: State the answer
    The child's weight is 343 N.

WORKED EXAMPLE 7: Calculating Spring Constant
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

PROBLEM:
A spring extends by 8 cm when a force of 12 N is applied.
Calculate the spring constant.

SOLUTION:

Step 1: List the known quantities
    Force, F = 12 N
    Extension, x = 8 cm = 0.08 m

Step 2: Write the equation for spring constant
              F
    k = ─────────
              x

Step 3: Substitute the values
              12
    k = ─────────
             0.08

Step 4: Calculate
    k = 150 N/m

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.6.1  VELOCITY IN CIRCULAR MOTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

RECALL:
  - Velocity is a VECTOR quantity
  - Velocity = speed + direction
  - If either speed OR direction changes, velocity changes

When an object travels in a circle:
  - It may move at constant SPEED
  - But its DIRECTION is always changing
  - Therefore, its VELOCITY is always changing

> KEY POINT:
> An object moving in a circle at constant speed is ACCELERATING because its
> velocity (direction) is constantly changing.


EXAMPLE: The International Space Station

The ISS orbits Earth at a constant speed of about 7660 m/s.
However, it is always changing direction as it orbits.
Therefore, its velocity is always changing.
This means the ISS is continuously accelerating.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.6.2  FORCE AND CIRCULAR MOTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Newton's first law tells us:
  - An object continues in a straight line at constant speed unless a
    resultant force acts on it

For an object to travel in a circle:
  - A force must act to change its direction
  - This force acts at 90° (perpendicular) to the direction of motion
  - The force is directed toward the CENTER of the circle

This inward force is called the CENTRIPETAL FORCE.

4.7.1  FRICTION IN SOLIDS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

> DEFINITION
> Friction is a force that works in opposition to the motion of an object.

Key points about friction:
  - Frictional forces SLOW DOWN the motion of objects
  - When friction occurs, energy is transferred by HEATING
  - Friction causes an increase in TEMPERATURE
  - The work done against frictional forces causes the temperature rise


CAUSE OF SOLID FRICTION:
  - Friction is caused by imperfections (bumps) in the surfaces of objects
    moving over one another
  - Even smooth surfaces have microscopic imperfections
  - These imperfections catch and resist motion


EFFECTS OF SOLID FRICTION:
  - Decreases the speed of the moving object
  - Increases the temperature of the objects due to heating


REDUCING SOLID FRICTION:
  - Lubricating the surfaces (oil, grease)
  - Smoothing the surfaces (polishing)

4.7.2  FRICTION IN FLUIDS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

DEFINITION OF FLUIDS:
  - Gases and liquids are known as FLUIDS
  - Fluids are different from solids because particles in fluids are free
    to move around

DRAG FORCE:
  - Friction acts on objects moving through fluids
  - Particles in the fluid collide with the object as it moves
  - This type of friction is called DRAG

AIR RESISTANCE:
  - Air resistance is a specific type of drag
  - It slows the motion of an object moving through air
  - Air particles bump into the object as it moves through the air

EFFECTS OF AIR RESISTANCE:
  - Reduces the speed of the object
  - Increases the temperature of the object and air due to heating

EXAMPLE: Space craft re-entry
When a spacecraft returns through the atmosphere:
  - Air resistance does work on the spacecraft
  - Energy is transferred to heat
  - The spacecraft's surface heats up dramatically
  - Heat shields are needed to protect the spacecraft


REDUCING FLUID FRICTION:
  - STREAMLINING the shape of the object
  - A streamlined shape allows the fluid to flow smoothly around it
  - This reduces the number of particle collisions

• A force is a push or pull that can change an object's speed, direction,
  shape, or size.

• The resultant force is the single force that represents all forces acting
  on an object combined.

• Balanced forces produce no resultant force; the object stays at rest or
  at constant velocity.

• Newton's first law: Objects remain at rest or at constant velocity unless
  a resultant force acts.

• Newton's second law: Acceleration is proportional to force and inversely
  proportional to mass (F = ma).

• Hooke's law: Extension is directly proportional to force up to the limit
  of proportionality (F = kx).

• In circular motion, velocity constantly changes (direction), so a force
  toward the center is required.

• Friction opposes motion, causes heating, and can be reduced by lubrication
  or streamlining.
