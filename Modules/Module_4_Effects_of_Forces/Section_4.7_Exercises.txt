            SECTION 4.7 FRICTION - PRACTICE EXERCISES (50 Questions)
================================================================================

Learning Objectives Covered:
  * Describe the effects of friction on motion.
  * Identify situations where friction is useful and where it is a problem.
  * Describe methods to reduce unwanted friction.
  * Describe methods to increase friction where needed.

Total Questions: 50
Assessment Objectives: AO1 (25%), AO2 (50%), AO3 (25%)

1. Friction is a force that:
   A: Always helps motion
   B: Opposes motion
   C: Acts in the direction of motion
   D: Only acts on stationary objects

   A: Cycling on a road
   B: A car engine is running
   C: A door hinge is moving
   D: An airplane is flying

   A: Increasing surface roughness
   B: Adding lubricant
   C: Increasing the force pressing surfaces together
   D: Using rubber instead of plastic

5. Streamlining reduces:
   A: Weight
   B: Air resistance
   C: Mass
   D: Gravity

6. Ball bearings reduce friction by:
   A: Increasing surface area
   B: Replacing sliding friction with rolling friction
   C: Making surfaces rougher
   D: Increasing weight

7. Which situation requires high friction?
   A: Skating on ice
   B: Car brakes
   C: Sliding down a slide
   D: Moving parts in an engine

8. Friction causes:
   A: Objects to cool down
   B: Energy to be transferred to heat
   C: Objects to gain mass
   D: Gravity to increase

9. Which is NOT a way to reduce friction?
   A: Lubrication
   B: Polishing surfaces
   C: Using ball bearings
   D: Increasing surface roughness

10. Tire treads are designed to:
    A: Reduce friction with the road
    B: Increase friction with the road
    C: Make the tire lighter
    D: Reduce air resistance
